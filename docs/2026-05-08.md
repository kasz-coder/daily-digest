**Training Data: "Anthropic's Boris Cherny: Coding's Printing Press Moment"**

**The Takeaway:** Anthropic's Boris Cherny, the creator of Claude Code, believes software development is already solved for him — he writes 100% of his code via Claude Code and runs thousands of agents overnight on his phone — and that coding is about to become as universal a skill as reading and writing.

Boris built Claude Code "accidentally" as part of Anthropic Labs, a small incubator team that created Claude Code, MCP, and the desktop app. For the first six months it barely worked. Growth didn't take off until Opus 4 in May, and it has inflected with every model release since. He describes the approach as "building for the next model" — they knew product-market fit wouldn't arrive for six months because they were building for capabilities the model hadn't yet reached.

His personal setup is extreme even by Anthropic standards: he does most of his work from his phone, running five to ten Claude Code sessions with hundreds of agents at any given moment. He uses `/loop` — which schedules a recurring job via Cron — for things like auto-rebasing PRs, fixing flaky CI tests, and pulling Twitter feedback every thirty minutes. "Loops are the future," he says. Routines (server-side loops) run even when he closes his laptop.

He predicts a "SaaSpocalypse" is unlikely because network effects and scale economies — the real moats — aren't going away. But switching costs and process moats will weaken as AI gets better at hill-climbing any target. Startups benefit most because they can build with AI natively, without fighting internal resistance to change. "It's the best time to build. It's the best time to be a startup."

On teams, he sees the rise of cross-disciplinary generalists: everyone on the Claude Code team codes — including the engineering manager, product manager, designers, and finance person. The model handles the technical execution; domain expertise is the differentiator.

His boldest analogy: coding is about to have its printing press moment. Before the printing press, 10% of Europeans were literate. After, literacy rose to 70% globally over centuries — but the cost of books dropped 100x in the first 50 years. Similarly, the best person to write accounting software today might be a great accountant, not an engineer, because knowing the domain is the hard part and coding is now the easy part. "It's going to be a skill like knowing how to send a text message."

On whether Claude Code's success is the model or the product: it's 50/50 today, but in a year the model will be so much better that safety harnesses (prompt injection checks, permission modes, human-in-the-loop) will matter less. "The model will just do the right thing."
