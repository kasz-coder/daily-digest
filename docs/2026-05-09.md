**The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck — "OpenAI Board Member Zico Kolter on the Real Risks of Frontier AI"**

Zico Kolter wears two hats that rarely coexist: OpenAI board member (chairing the Safety & Security Committee) and head of Carnegie Mellon's machine learning department. His core thesis challenges the prevailing assumption: bigger models don't automatically get safer.

The takeaway: AI safety doesn't come from scale. Despite training systems on more data and compute, robustness against jailbreaks and prompt injection hasn't improved proportionally. Kolter's team at CMU published the GCG jailbreak paper showing how easily frontier models can be manipulated — and how the AI labs responded by building state-of-the-art defenses. But he emphasizes this is an arms race, not a solved problem.

What makes Kolter unusual is his pragmatic stance. He's not a doomer who calls for six-month pauses, nor an accelerationist who dismisses risks. Instead, he frames AI safety as a global effort requiring collaboration between labs, researchers, and governments. His four categories of risk (capability, misuse, alignment, and systemic) provide a clearer taxonomy than the vague "AI extinction" framing.

Perhaps his most striking observation: "AI systems are incredibly simple. That entire set of code, probably two to 300 lines of Python code. That blows my mind. The entire complexity of an AI system evolves from the data they're trained on." This simplicity both excites and terrifies him — it means progress can come from unexpected directions, but also that understanding behavior is notoriously hard.

On agents: they're expanding the attack surface significantly because they take actions in the world, not just respond to queries. Whether they're ready for production depends heavily on the specific use case — some are, many aren't.
